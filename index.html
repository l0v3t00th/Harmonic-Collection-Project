INTERNET NARRENSCHIFF

Confined on the ship, from which there is no escape, the madman is delivered to the river with its thousand arms, the sea with its thousand roads, to that great uncertainty external to everything.

- Michel Foucault, Madness and Civilization: A History of Insanity in the Age of Reason

Internet Narrenschiff takes its name from the medieval concept of the “Narrenschiff”—the “Ship of Fools.” In European literature and art, the Narrenschiff was a vessel carrying society’s so-called “madmen” away from the city, a powerful metaphor for how communities once sought to exile or hide those who did not conform to prevailing norms.

Today, the same spirit of exclusion persists—only now it sails through digital waters. Our society continues to “evilize” mental illnesses, turning genuine conditions of human suffering into symbols of moral failure, danger, or weakness. Stereotypes and prejudices thrive in online discourse and popular culture, deepening the stigma that isolates those who struggle internally.

Internet Narrenschiff charts this troubled sea. It documents and exposes the common prejudices and cultural distortions surrounding mental illnesses—how they are misunderstood, demonized, and simplified into caricatures. But this project is not merely a critique: it is also a call for righteous recognition—to see mental illness not as a moral flaw, but as a profoundly human reality deserving of empathy, understanding, and dignity.

We navigate the myths, the fears, and the forgotten truths.
